About Me

My story

I started at Georgia State University’s Perimeter College, where I earned my Associate of Science in Engineering while working and staying involved on campus. In Summer 2026 I transferred to Georgia Tech to finish a Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering.

Before I designed circuits, I built them by hand. As a machinist at Roytec Industries, I assembled elevator wiring harnesses and conduits for custom industrial jobs. I worked with everything from tiny AWG 32 wire to heavy 535 MCM cable, and I learned to solder, strip, and seal to the exact standards on an engineering drawing. Every day, I saw how a small wiring decision affected the safety and reliability of a finished product. That job is what made me want to understand the “why” behind the systems I was building, and it’s why manufacturing and automation are where I want my career to go.

Today, my favorite way to learn is still with my hands. My current project is a portable guitar preamp that lets me practice without filling the house with sound. It started as a practical problem at home and turned into a lesson in analog circuits, op-amps, and tone shaping.

What I bring to a team

My CliftonStrengths top five are Context, Woo, Empathy, Communication, and Developer. In practice, that means I like to understand how something got the way it is before I try to change it, and I’m comfortable meeting new people and explaining technical ideas clearly.

I’ve put those strengths to work outside the classroom. As a Supplemental Instruction Leader for Multivariable Calculus for Engineers, I led weekly sessions for 10 to 20 engineering students and helped them work through problems together. As Traditions and Entertainment Director for the Panthers Activities Council, I helped plan and run campus events that brought students together.

Attending the 2026 NSBE Convention was a turning point. Being surrounded by so many driven engineers pushed me to think more seriously about my own career. I networked with students who share my excitement for engineering, talked with professionals, and saw career paths I hadn’t considered before.
