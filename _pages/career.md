<h1 class="post-headline">Career Goals</h1>

<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/career-roadmap.svg" alt="Timeline from Georgia State to Georgia Tech, a Summer 2027 internship, graduation in December 2028, and a career in automation and manufacturing">

## Where I want to go

My long-term goal is to work as an electrical engineer in **automation and manufacturing**, designing the control systems, sensors, and electronics that keep production lines running safely and efficiently. I want to be the engineer who can read a schematic, walk onto the factory floor, and understand how a design decision affects the people building the product.

My time at Roytec Industries showed me this side of engineering up close. I assembled harnesses to engineering drawings and saw how small details in wiring and assembly affected quality and safety. I want to move from building these systems by hand to designing and improving them.

## How I'll get there

**1. Build a strong circuits foundation (2026–2027).** At Georgia Tech, I'm focusing on the Electronic Devices and Circuit Technology threads. My upcoming courses include Microelectronic Circuits (ECE 3040), Signals and Systems (ECE 3084), and the Measurements, Circuits and Microelectronics Lab (ECE 3043). These cover the analog and measurement skills that industrial electronics depend on.

**2. Get industry experience (Summer 2027).** I'm applying for electrical and hardware engineering internships, with a focus on manufacturing, automation, and industrial companies. An internship will show me how engineering teams work in production and which skills employers need most.

**3. Go deeper in analog and devices (2027–2028).** I plan to take Analog Electronics (ECE 3400), Operational Amplifier Design (ECE 4435), Semiconductor Devices (ECE 3450), and IC Fabrication (ECE 4452). IC Fabrication in particular connects directly to my interest in how things are manufactured.

**4. Prove it in senior design.** In ECE Design Fundamentals (ECE 3011) and my culminating design course (ECE 4015), I want to work on a project with an automation or manufacturing focus.

**5. Graduate and launch my career (December 2028).** After earning my B.S. in Electrical Engineering, I plan to start as an electrical, controls, or manufacturing engineer and grow into leading automation projects.

## Skills I still need to build

My roadmap is strong on circuits, but automation also depends on control systems and industrial programming. To close those gaps, I plan to:

- Learn programmable logic controller (PLC) basics and ladder logic
- Study feedback control systems, possibly through the Robotics thread, which I'm also considering
- Build embedded projects that read sensors and drive motors, connecting my circuits knowledge to real-world control
- Stay active in NSBE to keep learning from engineers already working in industry
